#### 1. Decision Frameworks & Analytical Models
**Key Frameworks/Models:**
- **Heckscher-Ohlin (H-O) Theory**: An analytical model predicting the pattern of trade based on relative factor abundance (e.g., capital, labor) and factor intensity.
- **The Gravity Model of Trade**: Posits that trade flows are directly proportional to economic size (GDP) and inversely proportional to geographic distance, influenced by border effects, shared language, and history.
- **IS-LM-FE (Mundell-Fleming) Model**: A macroeconomic framework for the open economy to analyze internal (employment, price stability) and external (balance of payments) equilibrium under fixed/floating exchange rates.
- **The Eclectic Paradigm (OLI Framework)**: John Dunning's model explaining MNE existence through Ownership (Firm-Specific), Location, and Internalization advantages.
- **Porter’s Diamond & Cluster Theory**: Argues that regional competitive advantage lies in geographic concentrations of interconnected companies and institutions.
- **Computable General Equilibrium (CGE) Models**: Complex input-output models used for *ex-ante* analysis of trade agreements.
- **One-Dollar, One-Vote Metric**: Evaluates trade policy efficiency by weighting every dollar of gain or loss equally across consumers, producers, and government.

#### 2. Textbook vs. Reality
- **Textbook Free Trade vs. Geopolitical Reality**: Textbooks model free trade under competitive neutrality. Reality shows modern states constantly intervening using tariffs and non-tariff barriers for national security and strategic leverage.
- **CGE Long-Run Balance vs. Displacement Reality**: Models assume labor markets reach full employment naturally. In reality, trade policies create painful, long-lasting structural adjustments and worker displacement.
- **Factor-Price Equalization Theorem vs. Wage Gaps**: Stolper-Samuelson predicts wage equalization under free trade. In reality, transport costs, differing tech, and barriers prevent wage convergence.
- **The Leontief Paradox**: H-O predicted U.S. capital-intensive exports, but testing showed U.S. paradoxically exporting labor-intensive goods, highlighting the role of skilled labor.
- **Consumer Focus vs. Producer Reality**: Textbooks focus on aggregate consumer welfare, while political reality is dominated by intense sociotropic producer concerns about jobs.

#### 3. Managerial Insights
- **Geopolitical Risk Mitigation**: Geopolitics is a core corporate strategy concern. Managers must realign inventory, renegotiate supplier contracts, and consider reshoring to avoid tariff and supply network shocks.
- **Strategic Transfer Pricing**: Setting internal prices of goods/IP traded between global affiliates to shift reported profits from high-tax to low-tax jurisdictions.
- **First-Mover Advantage**: In industries with external economies of scale, first movers capture export markets and lock in permanent cost advantages.

#### 4. Professor's Rules (Always/Never)
- **ALWAYS use the Specificity Rule**: When addressing an economic distortion, intervene with the policy tool that acts as *directly as possible* on the source of the problem. Import barriers are rarely the most efficient tool.
- **ALWAYS evaluate the Hierarchy of Credibility**: Prioritize peer-reviewed academic journals over editorially reviewed magazines and unreviewed blogs.
- **NEVER treat international trade as a zero-sum game**: Trade is a positive-sum activity based on comparative advantage; one country's gain is not another's loss.
- **ALWAYS apply Mundell’s Assignment Rule**: In a fixed exchange rate regime, assign fiscal policy to domestic stabilization (internal balance) and monetary policy to balance of payments (external balance).
- **NEVER stop at descriptive analysis**: Analysts must move beyond describing a situation to analyzing the "mortar" (connections) between the "bricks" (facts).

#### 5. Common Mistakes
- **Conflating Current Account Deficits with "Losing"**: A deficit simply means a country is a net borrower, paying for goods by exporting paper/electronic IOUs representing claims on future goods.
- **Dismissing Job Losses as "Transition Costs"**: Displaced workers face life-long transitions; using academic jargon damages political support for trade.
- **Using Market Exchange Rates for Global Income Comparisons**: Fails to account for cheaper non-traded goods in developing nations. Use PPP-adjusted metrics.
- **Ignoring Endogeneity in Trade-Growth Regressions**: Simple correlation does not prove trade causes growth. Analysts must use instrumental variables (like geography) to isolate causality.

#### 6. Step-by-Step Protocols
- **Predicting Trade Patterns (Heckscher-Ohlin Model)**:
  1. Assess relative factor abundance of trading countries.
  2. Determine factor intensity of the goods.
  3. Conclude trade patterns: capital-abundant specializes in and exports capital-intensive goods.
- **Analyzing Devaluation Success (Marshall-Lerner Condition)**:
  1. Calculate price elasticity of demand for exports ($d_x$) and imports ($d_m$).
  2. Verify: devaluation improves current account only if $|d_x + d_m| > 1$.
  3. Account for "J-Curve" timing (short-term worsening before long-run improvement).
- **Addressing Transborder Pollution**:
  1. Identify source and nature of the externality.
  2. Implement direct pollution tax equal to Social Marginal Cost if globally cooperative.
  3. Use property-rights approach if direct tax is impossible.
  4. Retreat to second-best policies (like trade barriers) if cooperation fails.

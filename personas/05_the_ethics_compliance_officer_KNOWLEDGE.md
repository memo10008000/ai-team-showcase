## 1. The Core Frameworks
*   **PMI Ethical & Legal Risk Framework (PMI, 2026):** Categorizes the risks of AI implementation into 7 Ethical Risks (algorithmic bias/discrimination, misinformation/hallucinations, opacity/black-box decisions, labor rights/job displacement, psychological strain/manipulation, accessibility/inclusivity barriers, and privacy violations) and 5 Legal Risks (regulatory noncompliance, IP/copyright infringement, liability, discovery documentation, and contractual disputes).
*   **COPE Publication Ethics & Scientific Integrity (RSC, 2026):** The global standard governing academic publishing integrity. Mandates strict prohibition of salami slicing, gift/ghost co-authorship, paper mills, and unvetted GenAI co-authorship. Enforces mandatory disclosure of AI tools and restricts GenAI visual generation to **Licensed Platforms** (BRIA AI, Gemini, Firefly) to mitigate third-party copyright infringement liabilities.
*   **Indigenous Data Sovereignty (OCAP Principles):** Mandates that research touching First Nations/Indigenous lands, communities, or cultural heritage adhere to **Ownership, Control, Access, Possession**. Non-Indigenous researchers must ethically decouple or pivot research questions regarding Indigenous territories if authentic community partnership and consent are absent.
*   **The 5 Principles of AI Ethics**: A unified framework establishing that AI must adhere to **beneficence** (doing good), **non-maleficence** (avoiding harm), **autonomy** (respecting human decision-making), **justice** (ensuring fair outcomes), and **explicability** (intelligibility and accountability).
*   **Traditional Normative Ethical Theories**: Divided into **consequentialist** approaches (e.g., Utilitarianism, which maximizes collective welfare; Egoism, maximizing self-interest) and **non-consequentialist** approaches (e.g., Deontology/Ethics of Duties, which focuses on rules like the categorical imperative; Rights & Justice).
*   **Kantian Theory of Leadership**: A framework wherein leaders treat followers and corporate stakeholders as ends in themselves, rather than merely as a means to increase shareholder wealth. It requires inclusive participation and decisions governed by rules of justice.
*   **Pragmatic Sustainability**: Rooted in classical American pragmatism (Dewey, James, Peirce), this moral framework bridges means and ends by focusing on relentless experimentation, innovation, and long-term reflection to integrate environmental considerations without sacrificing competitive advantage.
*   **Hofstede’s Cultural Dimensions**: A taxonomy evaluating cross-cultural business ethics based on metrics of **individualism, uncertainty avoidance, masculinity, and power distance**.
*   **Organizational Culture Typologies**: Frameworks categorizing corporate environments, notably the **Market Culture** (driven by competition, market share, and profitability) and the **Hierarchy Culture** (founded on formal structure, control, and efficiency).

## 2. The Methodologies & Formulas
*   **The Explanation Game (iML)**: A formal methodology for interpretable machine learning where users ask "what-if" (w-questions) to evaluate a model. It plots explanations on a three-dimensional Pareto frontier balancing **accuracy ($ε_1$), simplicity ($ε_2$), and relevance ($ε_3$)**. The relevance is scored using the expected utility formula: $\mathbb{E}[u(a_i,C)|E] = \sum P(c_j|E)u(a_i, c_j)$.
*   **Four-Level Ethical Assessment**: A tiered protocol (from Zimmerli/Wolf) for evaluating corporate action resolving dilemmas top-down via: 1) formal principles, 2) regional principles, 3) professional principles, and 4) material values.
*   **Sustainable Economic Value Added (EVA)**: A formula adapted for sustainability investments: **EVA = (ROIC - WACC) x Invested Capital**. Managers justify environmental investments by demonstrating decreased risk to lower WACC, or increased revenue/decreased costs to drive up ROIC.
*   **AI4SG (AI for Social Good) Design Practices**: A 7-step standard operating procedure for AI development, demanding falsifiable requirements, incremental deployment, the removal of irrelevant dataset proxies to ensure situational fairness, and receiver-contextualized interventions.
*   **Sustainable Product Marketing Playbooks**: Methodologies for pricing and positioning:
    *   *Independence*: Temporary price premiums aimed at "green" (believer) and "blue" (agnostic) consumers.
    *   *Dissonance*: Charging a premium for sustainability despite a reduction in traditional performance.
    *   *Resonance*: Charging the highest premium because both sustainability and traditional performance are enhanced.

## 3. The Empirical Evidence
*   **The Body Shop International**: Founder Anita Roddick applied organizational virtues of excellence, community, and holism, proving that an enterprise can treat business as a force for social good first, and prioritize bottom-line profits second.
*   **Corporate Climate Governance**: A Ceres report on 100 global companies demonstrated that leaders like DuPont (which cut GHG emissions by 72% since 1990) and GE (investing heavily in "ecomagination") proactively generate lasting strategic advantage over industry laggards.
*   **Asea Brown Boveri (ABB)**: Under CEO Percy Barnevik, the firm successfully modeled Kantian "co-leadership", demonstrating that decentralized power and inclusive stakeholder participation unleash hidden organizational talent.
*   **Air Canada Chatbot Hallucination**: An empirical failure emphasizing the critical necessity of Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) oversight and comprehensive AI governance frameworks to prevent autonomous system errors from harming consumers.
*   **Toyota Prius & Oatly**: Real-world examples of the "Dissonance Playbook" in action, proving that companies can successfully market products with performance trade-offs if they leverage strong social signaling for target demographics.
*   **The 2008 Subprime Mortgage Crisis**: A systemic breakdown illustrating how moral recklessness, combined with skewed incentive-based compensation and complex information asymmetry, can trigger globally catastrophic financial consequences.

## 4. The Practitioner's Playbook
*   **PMI AI Ethical Risk Mitigation (PMI, 2026):** For any AI deployment, mitigate (1) bias by ensuring diverse training datasets and active bias auditing, (2) hallucinations through mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) overrides and data validation, and (3) opacity by rejecting "black-box" third-party algorithms unless vendor agreements guarantee audit rights and explainability benchmarks.
*   **PMI Legal Discovery & Liability Protocols (PMI, 2026):** Maintain rigorous, version-controlled records of AI training datasets, source validation, model configurations, and decision logs to meet legal discovery requirements in the event of litigation or regulatory investigation. Ensure contracts explicitly allocate liability and indemnification for AI-induced errors.
*   **Adopt Ethical Pluralism**: Do not rely on a single ethical theory; analyze moral dilemmas through multiple lenses (e.g., deontology, utilitarianism, and virtue ethics) to achieve a comprehensive, robust understanding of complex situations.
*   **Implement Pro-Ethical Design**: Architect systems that "nudge" users toward ethical choices while preserving their autonomy (e.g., deploying speed cameras that allow choice, rather than speed bumps that paternalistically force behavior).
*   **AI Risk Management Lifecycle**: Embed continuous risk controls into agile development. Define AI use cases clearly, conduct data privacy and bias assessments, mandate a manual "kill switch" for deployed algorithms, and regularly audit decision boundaries.
*   **Prioritize Organizational Integrity Over Compliance**: Shift from a fear-based, punishment-oriented legal compliance mindset to proactive integrity programs that foster a climate of trust, empower employees, and embed ethics into the core corporate value structure.
*   **Translate AI Principles to Practice**: Enforce algorithmic impact assessments, establish clear "red lines" for what tasks should not be delegated to AI, and mandate open, reproducible code to ensure traceability throughout the supply chain.

## 5. The Red Flags
*   **The 5 Malpractices of Digital Ethics**:
    *   *Ethics Shopping*: Retrofitting pre-existing behaviors by cherry-picking convenient ethical codes.
    *   *Ethics Bluewashing*: Making misleading claims about digital values to superficially appear ethical.
    *   *Ethics Lobbying*: Exploiting self-regulation to deliberately delay or water down necessary legal norms.
    *   *Ethics Dumping*: Exporting unethical data or research practices to regions with weaker legal frameworks.
    *   *Ethics Shirking*: Reducing ethical effort because the perceived return on investment is low.
*   **Agency Laundering & Automation Bias**: The severe risk of human decision-makers either blindly deferring to mechanized objectivity or distancing themselves from morally suspect outcomes by blaming the algorithm.
*   **The Bathsheba Syndrome**: The phenomenon where highly successful leaders suffer ethical failures due to complacency, inflated entitlement, and the abuse of organizational resources.
*   **Financial Sales Malpractices**: Be highly vigilant against **churning** (excessive trading to generate broker commissions), **unsuitability** (selling inappropriate grades/types of securities to clients), and **insider trading** (exploiting non-public information).

## ADVANCED ETHICS, GOVERNANCE, AND TECHNOLOGY UPDATE (AUG 2025 LECTURES)
**Source:** MGMT 566 Lectures 8 (Corporate Governance), 9 (Managing Business Ethics), and Unit 9 (Technology and Ethics)

### 1. Core Academic Frameworks & Theoretical Models
*   **The Principal-Agent Problem:** The inherent conflict of interest between shareholders (principals) and officers/managers (agents). When principals cannot effectively monitor, agents may act in self-interest (e.g., shirking, empire building, excessive risk-taking).
*   **Postmodern Issues Management:** Acknowledges that in a hyper-connected environment, reality is unstable and issues amplify rapidly. Distinguishes between "Noise" (which can be ignored) and "Problems/Opportunities" that require active engagement.
*   **Technological Utopianism vs. Dystopianism:** The tension between expecting technology to reduce hardship and discover new frontiers (Utopian) versus fearing a loss of cultural identity, spiritualism, and rising global elitism/dependence (Dystopian).

### 2. Key Methodologies & Formulas
*   **Social Accounting & Auditing:** The voluntary process to assess and communicate social, ethical, and environmental impacts. "Good" social accounting requires Inclusivity, Comparability, External verification, and Disclosure (Standards: SA 8000, ISO 14001, GRI, AA1000S).
*   **Four-Point Ethics Organization:** Structuring business ethics through Compliance orientation, External orientation, Values orientation, and Protection orientation.
*   **Governance Control Mechanisms:** The three solutions to the Principal-Agent problem: 1) Incentives (stock options/grants), 2) Internal Monitoring (Board of Directors), and 3) External Controls (market for corporate control, threat of takeovers).

### 3. Crucial Empirical Evidence & Case Studies
*   **University of Calgary Ransomware (2016):** Paid a $20,000 ransom to cybercriminals to protect world-class research data, highlighting the ethical dilemma of rewarding illegal behavior versus protecting institutional value.
*   **Theranos Scandal:** A prime example of prioritizing financial gain and media hype over product validation in healthcare, exposing massive failures in venture capital and media due diligence.
*   **Silk Road (Ross Ulbricht):** The dark web marketplace utilizing Bitcoin for illicit transactions, underscoring how legitimate innovations (cryptocurrency, anonymization) are rapidly co-opted for criminal enterprise.
*   **GM vs. Ralph Nader:** A textbook failure of Issues Management ("Negative Advocacy"). Instead of addressing Nader's auto-safety concerns, GM attacked his personal life, which backfired massively and damaged GM's reputation.

### 4. The Practitioner's Playbook (SOPs)
*   **Ethical Audit Execution:** When auditing a firm, systematically check: Income (honest forecasting), Workforce (health, safety, aesthetics), Customers (full disclosure, no planned obsolescence), Management Style (transparency), and Environment (waste, emissions).
*   **Constructive Issues Management:** Anticipate problems via environmental scanning. *Never* engage in negative "rival camp" advocacy or smear campaigns. Maintain dialogue with stakeholders and be willing to publicly admit fault.
*   **Designing Global Codes of Ethics:** Avoid vague statements. Maximize employee participation during the development stage to ensure "buy-in," follow through with strict discipline for breaches, and balance core human values with local traditions for multinational enterprises.

### 5. Critical Red Flags & Vulnerabilities
*   **Executive Compensation Risks:** Stock options have asymmetric incentives and can inadvertently encourage managers to take excessive risks, manipulate short-term accounting profits, or time the market at the expense of long-term R&D.
*   **Board Weaknesses:** Boards where the CEO is also the Chairman, where "outside" directors have personal ties to the CEO, or where directors lack a vested financial interest in the firm.
*   **The "Big Data" & AI Bias Threat:** Unfettered collection of consumer data raises severe privacy/ownership issues. Furthermore, human biases are often coded into Artificial Intelligence, creating systemic ethical risks in automated decision-making.

## 🏛️ AI GOVERNANCE CASE STUDY: SHADOW AI & SYSTEMIC FAILURES (JUNE 2026 UPDATE)
**Source:** MGMT566 Assignment 3 — Shadow AI in the Canadian Public Sector (Guillermo Granillo, 2026)

### 1. Additional Theoretical Frameworks
*   **Information Asymmetry & Invisible Risk Transfer (Akerlof 1970):** In technology deployment, a structural failure where citizens/users bear the complete risk of data exposure but possess zero information about it, while the deploying organization holds full knowledge of the system's unvetted status but fails to disclose it.
*   **CSR Decoupling:** The organizational and reputation capital damage caused by a structural gap between high-level public ethical commitments (e.g., G7 Hiroshima AI Process, OECD AI Principles) and the domestic operational/regulatory reality.
*   **Gunningham's Beyond-Compliance Spectrum:**
    *   *Opportunistic Mode:* Deploying systems to capture immediate productivity/modernization benefits while externalizing social, safety, and privacy costs onto users.
    *   *Committed / Environmental Leader Mode:* Proactively internalizing the full costs, risks, and compliance requirements of technology deployment before proceeding.

### 2. Empirical Case Study: The Ontario AI Strategy Failure (May 2026)
*   **The Breakdown:** The Ontario Public Service (OPS) deployed an AI Strategy in November 2024 but failed to implement binding accountability structures or security controls. 
*   **The Violations:**
    *   *Shadow AI:* Thousands of civil servants used unsecured, unapproved generative AI tools on government devices, leaking sensitive citizen data to third-party platforms.
    *   *Compliance Failure:* AI training was made voluntary, resulting in a microscopic 3% completion rate across the public service.
    *   *AI Scribe Hallucinations:* Unvetted AI medical transcription systems procured for family physicians fabricated clinical records (prescriptions, clinical referrals, and lab tests), endangering patient safety and exposing physicians to massive liability.

### 3. Practitioner's Playbook: Ethics & Compliance Rework
*   **Operationalize Ethics as a Service (Morley et al. 2021):** Translate abstract ethical codes into practical, auditable controls. High-level policies without binding, automated enforcement represent "ethics washing" and will fail.
*   **Enforce Fiduciary Duty Over Efficiency:** Under the Principal-Agent model (Jensen & Meckling), public sector agents must never substitute their own productivity/modernization interests for their fiduciary obligation to protect citizen principals. Citizens are ends in themselves (Kantian deontology), never tools for administrative speed.

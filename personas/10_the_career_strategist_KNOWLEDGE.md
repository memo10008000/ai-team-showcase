### 1. The Core Frameworks
*   **The Competence Equation & PEAKS:** Competence requires Knowledge, Capability, and Attitude [1, 2]. This is expanded in the PEAKS framework: Personal characteristics, Experience, Attitude, Knowledge, and Skill [3].
*   **The KSA Model:** A multi-faceted aspect of competence categorized into Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities [4].
*   **TEER Framework:** Canada’s National Occupational Classification (NOC) organizes jobs into six levels based on Training, Education, Experience, and Responsibilities [5, 6].
*   **Business Analysis Competency Model:** A five-point proficiency scale (General Awareness, Practical Knowledge, Skilled, Expert, Strategist) mapped across six Knowledge Areas such as Strategy Analysis and Solution Evaluation [7-11].
*   **Career Management Framework:** A cyclical, four-stage model for building career capital: Know Your Value, Explore Your Options, Plan Your Actions, and Create Your Future [12, 13]. 
*   **Planned Happenstance & DOTS Model:** Career theories emphasizing the active generation of opportunities through curiosity, persistence, flexibility, and risk-taking during transitions [14, 15].
*   **Ikigai Concept:** A mental model mapping purpose at the intersection of what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, and what you get paid for [16].
*   **Supply Chain Competency Triangle:** Built on 14 Foundational Attributes (e.g., Adaptability, Systems Thinking), 7 Integrative Competencies (e.g., Supply Chain Design), and 4 Functional Competencies (e.g., Warehousing) [17-20].
*   **Organizational Project Management Structures:** Frameworks dictate whether companies use Functional, Projectized, or Matrix (weak, strong, balanced) structures to execute operations [21, 22]. 
*   **PEST & STEEPLED Criteria:** Environmental scanning frameworks to evaluate Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legislative, Environmental, Ethical, and Demographic impacts on the market [23].

### 2. The Methodologies & Formulas
*   **Teck Resources Financial Ratios:**
    *   *Net debt to net debt-plus-equity ratio:* Net debt divided by the sum of net debt plus total equity [24].
    *   *Net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio:* Net debt divided by adjusted EBITDA for the trailing 12 months [24].
    *   *Adjusted net debt to capitalization ratio:* (Net debt + other financial obligations) divided by (total debt + equity + other obligations) [24].
*   **Accomplishment Statement Formula:** Power Verb + Action (Task/Context) + Result/How/Why [25]. 
*   **Cover Letter Headline Formula:** Number/Trigger word + Adjective + Keyword + Promise [26].
*   **The T-Chart Method:** An analytical methodology for job applications that maps job posting requirements (soft skills, technical skills, core values) on the left against specific evidence of the candidate's matching background on the right [27, 28].
*   **B.C. Labour Forecasting Methodology:** Predicts future skills demand by integrating regional macroeconomic models, industry employment models, and occupational models [29, 30].
*   **LinkedIn Summary Formula:** Write 1-2 sentences about who you are, 3-5 sentences regarding your experience and top skills, and 1-2 sentences detailing your future goals and how members can engage with you [31].
*   **Demand & Supply Balancing:** A step-by-step collaborative process to identify and measure gaps between demand and resources, resolving variances through pricing, warehousing, or outsourcing to optimize costs and assets [32, 33].
*   **Strategic Sourcing Process:** Utilize Porter’s five forces for market analysis, integrate the data into a sourcing strategy, generate a supplier profile, build a competitive network, negotiate, and implement agreements [34, 35].

### 5. The Red Flags
*   **Application Pitfalls:** Using generic greetings like "To Whom It May Concern" is unacceptable; always research the name of the hiring manager or recruiter [57]. Starting every cover letter paragraph with "I" indicates you are not writing from the perspective of what you can do for the employer [45].
*   **Resume Non-Compliance:** **Do not include your age, photo, family/health status, race, religion, gender, or sexual orientation on a Canadian resume** to protect your personal information [58]. 
*   **Empty LinkedIn Buzzwords:** Avoid using meaningless, contextless words like "innovative," "driven," "successful," and "motivated" on your profile, as they provide zero value to recruiters without empirical backing [59]. 
*   **Social Media Professionalism Risks:** Refraining from unprofessional posts, badmouthing past employers, or arguing in comment sections is crucial, as recruiters actively monitor this behavior [60]. Ensure privacy settings are strict to avoid inappropriate tagged photos [61].
*   **Isolated Project Management Implementation:** Implementing project management practices in isolated silos without proper top-management support and integration fails to produce measurable results, severely risking the credibility of the PM program [62, 63].
*   **Supply Chain Ingress/Egress Risks:** Organizations face significant risks of counterfeiting, theft, and copyright infringements; comprehensive threat assessments and legal deterrent instruments must be deployed across all partners [64, 65].
## 6. RRU Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) Expertise: Internships vs. Co-ops vs. Field Practice
As the Career Strategist, you must master the specific parameters of Royal Roads University's WIL programs to advise Guillermo and his potential employers accurately.

### The Differences in Completion Options
*   **Co-op Education:** Must be a **paid**, full-time experience lasting a minimum of **420-460 hours (12-14 weeks)**. Students are legally deemed employees under the Employment Standards Act. It integrates classroom learning into professional contexts through structured cycles of preparation and reflection.
*   **Internship:** Requires a minimum of **420 hours (12 weeks)**. Internships can be paid or unpaid. If unpaid, they must strictly align with BC Labour Standards and the student must secure alternative compensation (e.g., travel expenses, professional development, certifications). Unpaid interns are covered by university WCB insurance within BC.
*   **Field Practice:** A short-term, **75-100 hour** project-based educational experience. It is often completed concurrently with academic coursework rather than as a full-time work term.

### Employer Requirements (What to tell an Employer)
When Guillermo pitches an internship or co-op to a potential employer, the employer must agree to the following RRU requirements:
1.  **Organizational Stability:** The host must be an established company in business for at least 3 years with a minimum of 3 employees.
2.  **Mentorship & Supervision:** They must assign an English-speaking supervisor to provide formal, weekly (or ongoing) feedback, mentorship, and integrate the student into the team.
3.  **Meaningful Work:** The role must provide hands-on, productive work that encourages skills growth. It cannot be purely observational or exclusively entry-level menial tasks.
4.  **Evaluations & Administration:** The employer must complete a mid-term and end-of-term formal performance evaluation.
5.  **Site Visits:** The employer must accommodate an RRU site visit (virtual or in-person) and maintain clear communication channels with the university regarding the student's progress or any environmental concerns.

### Student Requirements (What Guillermo must do)
*   **The Rationale:** For both Co-ops and Internships, Guillermo must write a **300-500 word student rationale** attached to his contract. This rationale must substantiate exactly how the role validates his in-class academic learning, meets course requirements, and advances his specific career goals.
*   **Timing:** The position must begin within the first two weeks of the academic course start date.

## 7. Official Canadian IRCC Work Permit Statutory Rules & Evidence
*   **Off-Campus Work Limits (IRCC IRPR R186(v)):** Under Regulation R186(v) of the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations* (IRPR, updated Nov 8, 2024), full-time study permit holders enrolled in eligible DLIs may work up to **24 hours per week off-campus** during active academic sessions, and full-time (unlimited hours) during designated scheduled academic breaks.
*   **On-Campus Work Limits (IRCC IRPR R186(f)):** Under Regulation R186(f) of the IRPR, full-time study permit holders may work **full-time (unlimited hours)** on campus without a separate work permit.
*   **Legal On-Campus Definition:** IRCC legally defines "on-campus" as working physically within the geographic boundaries of the campus of the post-secondary institution where the student is enrolled (i.e. physically located in any building, research office, lab, or student facility on the Royal Roads University campus).

#### 1. Decision Frameworks & Analytical Models
**Key Frameworks/Models:**
- **The Rhetorical Situation (Aristotle)**: Every message is dynamically shaped by the intersection between: Speaker, Purpose/Context, and Intended Audience.
- **The Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)**: persusive top-down organization: Main point/Answer at the top, supported by logical arguments in the middle, and factual/empirical data at the base.
  - *Vertical Relationship*: Sets up a Question/Answer dialogue to pull the reader down through the logic.
  - *Horizontal Relationship*: Groups ideas deductively (successive steps) or inductively (like-kind factors).
  - *SCQA Introductory Flow*: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer.
- **RADAR Framework**: Evaluates source credibility across: Relevance, Authority, Date, Appearance, and Reason.
- **Divergent vs. Convergent Thinking**: Outlining workflow separating idea exploration (divergent) from evaluative selection based on constraints (convergent).
- **The "Oreo Cookie" Model**: Evidentiary integration: Claim (top cookie) $\rightarrow$ Quoted Evidence (filling) $\rightarrow$ Analysis connecting evidence to point (bottom cookie).
- **Neeley's Employee Response Matrix**: Plots Employee Buy-in against Belief in Capacity during language policy shifts (Inspired, Frustrated, Oppressed, Indifferent).
- **The Managerial Grid (Blake & Mouton)**: Concern for people vs. concern for production (e.g., Impoverished, Authority-Obedience, Country Club).

#### 1B. Narrative Changemaking, Public Narrative & Strategic Speechcraft (RRU Changemaking Ingestion)
- **The Narrative Paradigm (Walter R. Fisher)**: Humans evaluate communication not through abstract formal logic alone, but as storytelling beings (*homo narrans*). Every speech, pitch, or proposal must satisfy two tests:
  - *Narrative Probability (Coherence)*: Does the story hold together logically, free of internal contradictions and jarring behavioral jumps?
  - *Narrative Fidelity (Rings True)*: Does the narrative square with the audience’s lived experience, values, and sense of truth?
- **Public Narrative Architecture (Marshall Ganz / Harvard)**: Mobilizes collective action through three tightly linked movements:
  - *Story of Self*: Why am I personally called to this issue? (Formative choice points, lived values).
  - *Story of Us*: What shared values, mutual identity, and collective destiny unite this room?
  - *Story of Now*: What is the urgent challenge, what choice must we make right now, and what is our hopeful path forward?
  - *The Dramatic Engine*: Every persuasive narrative arc pivots on $\mathbf{Challenge} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Choice} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Action} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Outcome}$.
- **Equipment for Living (Kenneth Burke & Robert McKee)**: Stories are not frivolous entertainment; they are symbolic cognitive tools that provide audiences with strategies to navigate adversity, process uncertainty, and make sense of structural change.
- **Strategic & Subversive Humour in Leadership (Geo Takach)**:
  - *The Borge Axiom*: *"Laughter is the shortest distance between two people."*
  - *Disarming Dogma*: Strategic humour lowers psychological threat, diffuses polarization, punctures corporate hubris, and enables teams to address the "elephant in the room" without triggering retributive conflict.
  - *5-Part Speechcraft*: $\text{Defining} \rightarrow \text{Structuring} \rightarrow \text{Humouring} \rightarrow \text{Crafting} \rightarrow \text{Delivering}$.
- **Indigenous Storywork & The Power of Story (Jo-ann Archibald / Harold Johnson)**: Upholds the 7 principles (Respect, Responsibility, Reciprocity, Reverence, Holism, Interrelatedness, Synergy). Treats cultural stories and local histories as living legal, ethical, and community covenants, recognizing that the stories we choose to tell construct the reality we inhabit.
- **The Triangled Connection in Public Address (Geo Takach)**: A speaker's persuasive authority rests on dynamically linking three points: **Audience $\\leftrightarrow$ Topic $\\leftrightarrow$ Speaker Themselves**, anchoring message credibility in narrative fidelity rather than abstract dogma.

#### 2. Textbook vs. Reality
- **Angelic Leadership vs. Destructive Reality**: Texts treat leaders solely as moral "white angels." Real-world executives ("grey suits") show destructive action, such as pseudotransformational leadership and goal sabotage.
- **Bureaucratic Controls vs. Social Learning**: Texts suggest rule-based risk management. Reality shows command-and-control increases transaction costs; true resolution requires social/normative internalization of values.
- **Chronological vs. Pyramid Presenting**: Standard storytelling moves from exposition to climax. Executive reality demands the climax/answer first, followed by arguments.

#### 3. Managerial Insights
- **Adaptive Communication in Remote Teams**: Dynamically adjusting strategies, exhibiting empathy, and leveraging digital tools to counter professional isolation.
- **Interceding in the "Blame Game"**: Fostering active listening and enforcing neutral, non-aggressive language early to halt team conflicts.
- **Englishnization Management**: Corporate language mandates require leaders to act as enforcers, but also provide verbal persuasion and learning opportunities to build confidence.
- **The Power of Effective Apologies**: Takes personal responsibility ("I" statements), avoids "ifs/buts", and defines concrete restitution.

#### 4. Always/Never Rules
- **ALWAYS analyze the audience first**: define their agenda, fears, needs, biases, and disposition before drafting.
- **ALWAYS include signal phrases**: Smith (2021) stated... and always include author, year, and page/paragraph for direct quotes.
- **ALWAYS reaffirm the thesis in the conclusion**: End papers with a clear restatement of your central position.
- **NEVER introduce new ideas or evidence in the conclusion**: Keep the ending strictly focused on synthesizing existing points.
- **NEVER use source wording without quotation marks**: Enforce absolute academic integrity.
- **NEVER let a quote stand alone as a sentence**: Integrate every citation smoothly with your own words.

#### 5. Common Mistakes
- **"Arm Waving" (Excessive Meta-Discourse)**: Announcing what you are doing (e.g., "This essay will show...") rather than simply making the point.
- **Pretentious and Complex Jargon**: Using obscure words (plethora, inexorable) when clarity and concision are preferred.
- **Drafting Paragraphs inside an Outline**: Outlines must be short, precise claims and cited quotes; drafting fully defeats the planning phase.
- **Weak Rebuttals**: Acknowledging a counterargument without demonstrating its structural weakness destroys the validity of your own case.
- **Rhetological Fallacies**: Cherry-picking evidence (confirmation bias), presenting false dilemmas, or using hasty generalizations.

#### 6. Step-by-Step Protocols
- **The 8 Steps of an Argumentative Paper**:
  1. Determine prompt parameters (word count, components).
  2. Discover information (preliminary research).
  3. Brainstorm (divergent thinking).
  4. Find relevant, credible evidence (RADAR).
  5. Sort information (convergent thinking).
  6. Outline using the Pyramid Principle.
  7. Write the paper.
  8. Revise and edit (correctness and style).
- **Counterargument & Rebuttal Tactics**:
  - *Tactic 1 (Alternative)*: Acknowledge the alternative $\rightarrow$ Highlight the specific gaps in that perspective.
  - *Tactic 2 (Own Flaw)*: Openly acknowledge your own risk/flaw $\rightarrow$ Provide evidence of how you mitigate it.
- **Sonnenfeld & Ward's Bouncing Back Protocol**:
  1. Choose to fight rather than take flight.
  2. Recruit others to your cause to regain status.
  3. Prove competence by delivering visible results.
  4. Rediscover your core mission and legacy.
- **Style and Concision Protocol**:
  1. Use specific, concrete language.
  2. Eliminate redundancies (e.g., change "due to the fact that" to "because").
  3. Avoid unnecessarily complex or pretentious words.
  4. Use action verbs in the active voice.

## IX. Quick Reference: APA 7th Edition Essentials

### In-Text Citations
- Author-date: (Smith, 2023) or Smith (2023) states...
- Direct quote: (Smith, 2023, p. 45)
- Multiple authors 3+: (Smith et al., 2023)

### Reference List Format
**Journal article:** Author, A. A. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name, Volume*(Issue), page–page. https://doi.org/xxxxx

**Book:** Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle*. Publisher.

**Website:** Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *Title of page*. Site Name. URL

### Common Errors to Avoid
- Running references together without hanging indent
- Capitalizing all words in article/chapter titles (only first word + proper nouns)
- Forgetting DOI when available
- Using first names instead of initials

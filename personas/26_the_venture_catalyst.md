## III. Always / Never Rules

- **ALWAYS validate the Problem before building the Solution**: Never write code, design complex database schemas, or build architecture diagrams until customer discovery proves people are actively suffering from the problem and spending resources on workarounds.
- **ALWAYS follow "The Mom Test" interviewing rules**: Never ask hypothetical questions like *"Would you buy this if we built it?"* Ask about past actions, current spending, and what happened the last time they experienced the pain point.
- **ALWAYS prepare a 3-point agenda for Mentor Power Sessions**: Never meet with a REV mentor without issuing a written agenda at least 24 hours prior highlighting your top 1–3 specific challenges.
- **ALWAYS define what specific result would change your mind before testing**: Establish the disconfirmation threshold in advance to prevent confirmation bias and data cherry-picking.
- **ALWAYS align Purpose with verifiable metrics via the Intent $\rightarrow$ Action $\rightarrow$ Evidence triad**: Ground every social or environmental claim in verifiable operational behaviors and tracked metrics.
- **ALWAYS stack Canadian non-dilutive grants first**: Maximize NRC IRAP, Innovate BC, and Mitacs funding to build and de-risk technology before giving away founder equity.
- **NEVER fall into the "Premature Scaling" trap**: Never spend money on branding, swag, expensive legal incorporation, or sales software before achieving Problem-Solution Fit.
- **NEVER accept vanity metrics**: Reject social media likes, pageviews, or verbal compliments as proof of traction. Traction is validated only by committed time, data access, letters of intent (LOIs), or cash deposits.
- **NEVER build on unvalidated beliefs**: Convert every belief into a testable assumption, run an experiment, collect traceable evidence, and only then make a decision ($\text{Belief} \rightarrow \text{Assumption} \rightarrow \text{Test} \rightarrow \text{Evidence} \rightarrow \text{Decision}$).
- **NEVER ignore collateral or systemic impacts touching non-customers**: Run the "Before You Build" 6-question gate to verify whether unintended side effects harm community health, local economies, or natural ecosystems.
- **NEVER build purely extractive or hyper-financialized ventures**: Ensure civic and early-stage ventures solve real human needs and foster local community vitality, resisting the creation of manufactured wants that deplete local social or natural capital (Pollock & Rotering).
- **NEVER hide behind passive founder behavior**: Mentors and REV staff provide advice, not solutions. The entrepreneur is the driver who owns communication, scheduling, and risk.

## IV. Step-by-Step Practitioner Protocols

### Protocol 1: Rapid Lean Canvas Sprint (Weeks 1 & 2 REV Homework)
1. **Timebox to 30 Minutes:** Draft Version 1.0 of the 9-box Lean Canvas without agonizing over perfection.
2. **Anchor the Top 3 Problems:** Identify who feels the pain most acutely (Early Adopters) and what existing alternatives they use today.
3. **Formulate the UVP:** Apply the formula: *[Clear Outcome] + [Specific Timeframe / Metric] + [Without the Primary Friction / Objection]*.
4. **Identify the Top 2 Riskiest Assumptions:** Circle the 2 assumptions that, if false, immediately kill the business.
5. **Formulate Weekly Test Cards:** Design a 48-hour experiment to test each assumption (e.g., 5 discovery interviews, landing page smoke test).

### Protocol 2: Bi-Weekly Group Mentorship Pod Script (3-Minute Update)
- **Min 0:00 – 0:45 (Wins & Momentum):** "Over the last two weeks, our major win was [Metric/Milestone achieved, e.g., 8 interviews conducted, MVP prototype tested with 2 local businesses]."
- **Min 0:45 – 1:45 (Current Trajectory & Learning):** "Our Lean Canvas assumption was [X]. Customer feedback proved that [Insight/Pivotal learning], so we adjusted our UVP to [Y]."
- **Min 1:45 – 2:30 (Bottleneck Challenge):** "Our #1 bottleneck right now is [Specific operational, regulatory, or technical hurdle]."
- **Min 2:30 – 3:00 (The Ask):** "My specific ask for this pod or our mentors is [Introduction to specific persona, feedback on pilot agreement, or testing an assumption]."

### Protocol 3: REV 10-Slide Pitch Night Deck Construction
1. *Slide 1 (Hook):* Venture Name + One-sentence thesis + Founder Superpower.
2. *Slide 2 (Problem):* The painful, costly Westshore/regional challenge (grounded in real user stories).
3. *Slide 3 (Status Quo Failure):* Why existing alternatives fail to solve it.
4. *Slide 4 (Solution & UVP):* What the venture delivers and how it fundamentally alters the outcome.
5. *Slide 5 (MVP & Architecture):* Prototype screenshots, process workflow, or architecture schematic.
6. *Slide 6 (Market Opportunity):* Beachhead segment $\rightarrow$ Total Addressable Market (TAM).
7. *Slide 7 (Business Model):* Pricing, revenue streams, unit economics ($\text{LTV}/\text{CAC}$).
8. *Slide 8 (Traction & Validation):* Interview count, LOIs, pilot commitments, customer quotes.
9. *Slide 9 (Team):* Founders, complementary strengths, RRU/REV ecosystem backing.
10. *Slide 10 (The Ask & Impact):* Clear ask (pilot partners, angel round, mentor intros) + Community/Ecosystem ROI.

### Protocol 4: Purpose & Impact Statement Formulation (Module 4 Deliverables)
1. **Purpose Statement Assembly:**
   $$\text{"We exist to [core operational offering] for [target beneficiary/customer] so that [systemic difference or transformation]."}$$
   - *Example:* "We exist to deploy autonomous diagnostic intelligence for independent community operators so that regional enterprises maintain sovereign economic vitality against centralized monopolies."
2. **Impact Statement Assembly:**
   $$\text{"Our venture aims to create [concrete measurable change] for [stakeholder/community], measured through [primary verification indicator]."}$$
   - *Example:* "Our venture aims to eliminate operational carbon waste across South Island hospitality logistics, measured through tonnes of landfill diverted and local supply chain spend retention."
3. **The Alignment Chain Verification:**
   - Define **Intent** $\rightarrow$ define concrete **Action** $\rightarrow$ establish verifiable **Evidence**.

### Protocol 5: "What Result Would Change Your Mind" Hypothesis Sprint (Module 3)
1. **Isolate the Lethal Assumption:** Identify the single assumption that is simultaneously high-consequence and low-evidence.
2. **Decompose from Belief to Testable Hypothesis:** Convert *"People will want this"* into *"At least 4 out of 10 interviewed operations managers in Greater Victoria will confirm spending >$500/month or >5 hours/week on this specific workaround."*
3. **Establish the Disconfirmation Threshold:** Explicitly define prior to testing: *"If fewer than 3 operators confirm this pain, we will kill or fundamentally alter this feature."*
4. **Select Minimum Credible Experiment:** Choose the lowest-cost, fastest experiment from the Evidence Spectrum (interviews, smoke test landing page, manual concierge service).
5. **Trace Provenance:** If using AI for secondary discovery (Reddit, reviews), preserve verbatim quotes and verifiable source links without synthetic distortion.

### Protocol 6: "Before You Build" Ethical Gate (Module 4 Reflection)
1. **Run the 6-Question Reflection:**
   - What is the venture's purpose? Who is it for?
   - Who else is nearby or touched by it?
   - Does it impact the living environment or local civic economy?
   - Is it altering something for someone without explicit intent?
   - Is that unintended effect helping or hurting?
2. **Execute the Trade-Off Audit:** If an externality is negative, reject rationalization. Either re-architect the business model to eliminate the negative externality or explicitly halt the sprint until mitigated.

### Protocol 7: The Purpose Decision Filter & "Narrow & Deep" Audit (Kendall Gross Standard)
1. **Apply the Decision Filter:** For any major business decision (hiring, supplier selection, pricing model, tech architecture), ask:
   $$\text{"Is there a way to make this decision that makes sound business sense AND better reflects what we stand for?"}$$
2. **Audit Dispersion vs. Depth:** Avoid the "inch deep, mile wide" trap (generic, scattershot initiatives). Concentrate capital and effort into 1–2 high-depth capacity partnerships where systemic local impact is measurable.
3. **Harmonize Profit and Purpose:** Treat profit not as a competing goal, but as the essential fuel required to sustain and scale the venture's purpose over multi-decade horizons.

### Protocol 8: The Outside Advisory "Haircut" Audit & Anti-Focus List (Toby Barazzuol Standard)
1. **The Anti-Focus List:** Purpose must define what you will **NOT** do. Explicitly list 3 tempting market opportunities or client requests that the venture will actively reject because they dilute focus or violate core purpose.
2. **The "Outside Haircut" Protocol:** Never draft or pivot social purpose in an isolated silo ("cutting your own hair"). Always submit your Purpose Statement, Value Proposition, and Lean Canvas to an external facilitator, EIR, or mentor to uncover blind spots and stress-test assumptions.
3. **The Survival Baseline:** Treat financial viability and cash runway as the foundational prerequisite metric: without business survival, no purpose can be realized.

### Protocol 9: Navigating the BC 7-Step Venture Launch & BizPaL Permitting Sequence
1. **Validate & Plan:** Complete Problem-Solution validation and financial projections before committing legal registration capital.
2. **Structure & Professional Consultation:** Select structure (Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, Corporation, Society) and consult legal/CPA partners (e.g., Baker Tilly Victoria) for shareholder agreement hygiene.
3. **Name Approval:** Submit Name Request via BC Registries (`bcregistry.gov.bc.ca`) or Service BC (secures 56-day reservation).
4. **Provincial Incorporation & BN Issuance:** Complete incorporation via BC Registries to receive the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA.
5. **Municipal Licensing via BizPaL (`bizpal.ca`):** Input the venture's target municipality (Langford, Colwood, View Royal, etc.) and industry sector to generate the comprehensive checklist of municipal permits, fire code reviews, Island Health inspections, and signage approvals.
6. **Statutory Tax & WorkSafe Accounts:** Establish PST (BC Ministry of Finance), GST (CRA, if >$30k revenue), Corporate Income Tax, and WorkSafeBC coverage.
7. **Small-Business Advantage Stacking:** Evaluate Chamber benefits (Chambers Plan pooled group health benefits, Peloton Technologies payment rates, RRU WIL student co-ops, WorkLink wage subsidies).

### Protocol 10: Module 05 Customer Discovery, Buying Role Architecture & Decision Thresholds
1. **Hypothesis Standard Assembly:**
   $$\text{"We believe [customer group] in [situation] experience [problem] and want [outcome]. Our concept would help by [proposed solution]."}$$
   - *Constraint:* Keep the proposed solution strictly provisional; anchor the sprint to verifying the reality of the problem and the current workaround.
2. **Market Sizing vs. Demand Gate:**
   - Proving that a market exists (e.g., Statistics Canada, industry directories) does not prove demand for the specific offer. Treat market scans as upper boundaries, not proof of traction.
3. **Traceable Secondary Pain Point Discovery (Zero Synthetic Feedback):**
   - When extracting customer pain points via AI from public forums (Reddit, reviews), enforce exact provenance: raw quotes, dates, and original URLs. Reject synthetic, hallmarked, or generalized summaries.
4. **The 4 B2B Buying Roles Mapping:**
   - Always map the target account across all four roles: **User** (daily workflow experience), **Influencer** (technical/sustainability advisor), **Buyer** (budget holder signing checks), and **Approver/Blocker** (privacy, legal, cybersecurity gatekeepers under BC PIPA/OCAP).
5. **Skin-in-the-Game Validation:**
   - Reject vanity metrics (clicks, survey praise, polite encouragement). Validation requires a skin-in-the-game commitment: paid pilot, signed LOI, financial deposit, or sustained repeat usage.
6. **Pre-Set Decision Thresholds & 4-Way Gating:**
   - Define the quantitative pass threshold *before* launching the experiment: *"Before starting, we must see [X] to justify [Y]."*
   - At review date, enforce one of four unambiguous paths: **Continue, Revise, Stop, or Investigate Further**.

### Protocol 11: Veteran Trench Heuristics & The Anti-Commoditization Guardrail
1. **The Dragons' Den Fallacy (Peter Heuristic):**
   - Decenter personal founder passion from market value. Do not spend capital building products based solely on nostalgia or self-interest. In complex B2B systems, co-create alongside an anchor customer who dictates the real value proposition during development.
2. **The "Bag of Chips" Commoditization Test (Dan Heuristic):**
   - If a competitor can undercut you by 2 cents on a commodity widget, you do not have a defensible business. Build proprietary IP, deep relational moats, or sustainability data integration that cannot be easily copied. Master every operational task yourself first, then build systems to replace yourself so you can scale.
3. **The Mothership Principle (Calen McNeil Heuristic):**
   - When margin compression, inflation, or IT capital expenditures create friction with social/environmental purpose, *the mothership is purpose*. Compromising purpose destroys customer trust and team culture. Verify all environmental claims firsthand (e.g., Fisher Road composting) before making public claims.

### Protocol 12: Visitide Algorithmic Safeguards — The Paradox of Resentment & Threshold Economics
1. **The Paradox of Resentment Guardrail (Thomlinson / Doxey):**
   - When Visitide's GEO engine disperses visitor demand away from congested tourism corridors, it must prevent the **Paradox of Resentment**: dispersing tourists into residential or rural neighborhoods can trigger acute local annoyance (traffic, parking, noise, loss of quiet) just as severely as isolated resort enclaves breed resentment over exclusion. Visitide algorithms must monitor resident sentiment thresholds and avoid creating secondary irritation zones.
2. **Weaver & Lawton (2002) Threshold Integration:**
   - Dispersal routing must be calibrated against localized ecological and municipal **Thresholds** (water treatment caps, parking space limits, micro-trail erosion limits) rather than merely routing visitors toward smaller operators who may lack capacity or modern infrastructure.
3. **Plugging Locality Leakage (The Kreutzwiser-Murphy-Page Multiplier):**
   - Visitide's core value proposition to regional Destination Management Organizations (DMOs) and municipalities is tracking and expanding **Direct, Indirect, and Induced local spend retention** while plugging structural leakages (foreign MNC platform capture, pre-paid package extraction), demonstrating measurable local economic retention.

### Protocol 14: Customer Validation & Wedge Scoring Protocol (REV Module 06 & Nanda Playbook)
1. **The 3-Act Validation Meeting Structure (REV Module 06):**
   - **Act 1 — Lived Experience & Past Behavior (10–15 min):** Never pitch upfront. Probe the last time the problem occurred, specific costs in time/money/opportunity, and existing manual workarounds.
   - **Act 2 — Business Proposition & Friction Probing (5–10 min):** Deliver the 30–60 second proposition (*"We help [customer] achieve [outcome] through [product]. Compared with [alternative], our offer provides [benefit]"*). Immediately probe friction: *"What makes this difficult to adopt or buy? What would you use instead?"*
   - **Act 3 — The Commitment Ask / Next Step (2–5 min):** Request a concrete commitment (book demo, pilot test, formal quote, price verification). **The Golden Validation Rule:** *Positive comments and hypothetical purchase intentions do not establish demand. A declined next step is useful evidence too. Record what they actually agree to do and by when.*
2. **The 2-Minute REV Synthesis Pitch:**
   - Report validation findings to cohort peers and EIRs using the strict 4-part formula:
     $$\mathbf{"We\ believed\ [\_].\ We\ spoke\ with\ [\_].\ We\ observed\ [\_].\ We\ will\ now\ [\_]."}$$
3. **VentureWell Scientific Hypothesis Syntax:**
   - Structure every assumption before testing as:
     $$\mathbf{[Specific\ Role]}\ \text{makes a}\ \mathbf{[Decision\ or\ Behavior]}\ \text{that allows for a}\ \mathbf{[Quantified\ Metric]}$$
     Refine until it has a quantifiable pass/fail threshold (e.g., *"Boutique hotel general managers require a 25% reduction in channel commission fees to adopt a direct booking tool"*).
4. **Segment Wedge Scoring & Cadence (Nanda, 2026):**
   - Conduct 30 discovery interviews across 3 candidate segments (10 per segment: *patterns at 10, themes at 20, conviction at 30*).
   - Rank target segments using the Wedge Equation:
     $$\mathbf{\text{Wedge Score}} = \mathbf{\text{Pain Frequency}} \times \mathbf{\text{Urgency of Workaround}} \times \mathbf{\text{Willingness-to-Pay}}$$
     Pick the sharpest cut, not the largest total addressable market.
5. **The 40/20 Synthesis Filter & Traceable PRD:**
   - Themes present in $>40\%$ of interviews with active workarounds are **REAL** (build and validate). Themes $<20\%$ are **NOISE** (discard).
   - *PRD Traceability Mandate:* Every feature, user story, or architectural component in the PRD must be directly traceable to an interview quote or observed workaround. If it cannot be traced, cut it.
6. **Execution Discipline & The Awkward Silence:**
   - Maintain 5–7 seconds of silence after asking open-ended questions; allow the customer to break the pause with operational truth.
   - Avoid the 6 Deadly Sins: Death by Demo, Death by PowerPoint, Passion Blindness, Pitching instead of listening, Asking about future predictions, and Solo interviewing without verbatim notes.

### Protocol 13: Visitide Algorithmic Capacity Hard-Caps — The Infrastructure-Visitor Threshold Axiom
1. **The Infrastructure-Visitor Threshold Axiom (Guillermo Granillo Sanchez, Nov 2, 2026):**
   - In constrained geographic and historical corridors (e.g., Southern Vancouver Island's Malahat Highway and Douglas Street arterial): physical terrain and existing residential/commercial buildings make road expansion physically impossible or socially unacceptable (requiring expropriation of homes and destruction of neighborhoods). Air transport causes severe emissions, while marine ferries are slow and throughput-constrained.
   - *The Foundational Axiom:* **"Maybe the solution is to try to receive only the number of visitors that the infrastructure is capable to support."**
2. **From Soft Nudges to Algorithmic Hard-Caps:**
   - Traditional travel tech relies solely on dynamic pricing or gentle suggestions. Visitide implements an **Algorithmic Hard-Cap Gatekeeper** tied directly to real-time physical infrastructure throughput (highway congestion levels, ferry vehicle deck saturation, emergency healthcare wait times, and municipal water flow).
   - When localized capacity breaches defined ecological or civil thresholds (Weaver & Lawton, 2002), Visitide automatically throttles new reservation intakes, alerts municipal DMOs, and dynamically diverts incoming visitor flows to secondary micro-corridors before gridlock occurs.
3. **Multi-Stakeholder Wicked Problem Governance:**
   - Grounded in Rittel & Webber (1973), overtourism is treated as a wicked problem with no single linear fix. Visitide's B2B architecture bridges municipal authorities, private operators, transit agencies, and local residents through transparent, real-time shared capacity dashboards.

### Protocol 15: Visitide Algorithmic Routing Engine & Peak-Load Pricing (The ArchaeoQuest & Galápagos Models)
1. **The ArchaeoQuest GIS Dynamic Routing Engine (Eagles et al., 2002):**
   - Grounded in the Scottish ArchaeoLink Prehistory Park benchmark, Visitide's routing algorithms evaluate user inputs: Party Size (1 to $>20$), Fitness/Mobility level, Transport Mode (Foot, Transit, EV, Coach), Time Budget ($<1$ hr to full-day), and Travel Intent.
   - The engine checks real-time parking, trail erosion, and municipal utility thresholds; instead of simply rejecting visitors when a corridor is saturated, it dynamically generates customized alternative itineraries redirecting visitor footfall to under-utilized secondary corridors.
2. **Marginal Cost & Peak-Load Congestion Pricing:**
   - Operationalizing the environmental economics formula:
     $$P_{\text{peak}} = MC_{\text{operating}} + MC_{\text{capacity expansion}} \quad \text{where} \quad |\epsilon_d| < 1$$
   - During peak congestion periods, Visitide's platform implements peak pricing surcharges that smooth temporal demand and generate dedicated capital expansion funds for local infrastructure without depressing overall economic throughput.
3. **The Galápagos 1993 Special Law Revenue-Retention Mechanism:**
   - Breaking the "Hyper-Centralized Revenue Siphoning" trap by ensuring Visitide's SaaS platform routes 45% of platform fees directly into local park/trail stewardship operations and 30% into municipal conservation infrastructure, creating a bulletproof, self-sustaining B2B value proposition for regional DMOs and First Nations.
